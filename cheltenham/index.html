The Track

Four days, 27 races and two courses – the Old Course and the New Course.

Read more

Cheltenham Facts:

  • During the Festival, 200 tonnes of divot mix will be used in treading / repairing the course and 100 sacks of grass seed.
  • There are 10 miles of running rail on the racecourse with 165 hurdles and 24 fences - measuring a quarter-mile long if they were all joined together.
  • After the Festival, there are around 4 million hoofprints on the racecourse!

The Jockeys

Someone who rides horses in horse racing or steeplechase racing, primarily as a profession.

Read more

Ruby Walsh – The all-time record holder for the most festival winners and has been leading jockey at the festival on eight occasions – including the last two years.

Festival Memory: Kauto Star became the first horse to regain the Gold Cup under Walsh in 2009, beating stablemate and defending champion Denman into second spot.

Barry Geraghty – Stable jockey to Nicky Henderson, Geraghty is assured a plum book of festival rides. He has been leading jockey at the festival twice.

Festival Memory: Sprinter Sacre’s Queen Mother Champion Chase win in 2013 was breathtaking. The Black Aeroplane was hard on the bridle as the trounced his rivals adding to his win in the Arkle a year earlier.

AP McCoy – Has been UK Champion jumps jockey for 19 seasons in a row but he tends to find Cheltenham winners hard to come by. Taquin Du Seuil provided his only success last year.

Festival Memory: Known for his never-say-die attitude, McCoy’s 2009 win on Wichita Lineman was as improbable as it was impressive. It remains one of the truly outstanding ‘McCoy Moments’.

Sam Twiston-Davies – Aged 22 he is in his first season as retained jockey for Paul Nicholls. The talented young rider can call on the Ditcheat firepower to boost his winning chances.

Festival Memory: Few have more belief in the 2013 Neptune winner The New One than father and son combination Nigel and Sam Twiston-Davies. Can he land this year’s Champion Hurdle after an unlucky third last year?

Bryan Cooper – The new kid on the block for the powerful Gigginstown House Stud, Cooper (22) burst on the scene with three festival wins in 2013. A fall in the Fred Winter last year cut his festival short and Cooper will have high hopes this time around.

Festival Memory: Our Conor’s runaway success in the Triumph Hurdle in 2013 really announced Cooper on the big stage.

One to Watch 2015: Kings Palace, RSA Chase, Day Two

Previous festival form is a crucial factor to consider when assessing the chances of any horse. Here are some winners from the 2014 Festival to look out for:

Vautour: Winner of the Supreme Novices’ Hurdle last time, he has shown plenty promise over fences and targets the JLT Novices’ Chase on Day Three

Read more

Day One:

The Cheltenham Roar – the legendary seal of approval from the packed grandstands that greets the start of the Supreme Novices’ Hurdle.

Hat-trick Seekers – Willie Mullins and Ruby Walsh have teamed up to win the festival curtain raiser in the last two years. They bid for the hat-trick with leading fancy Douvan.

Irish Eyes Are Smiling – Ireland enjoyed six winners on the closing afternoon of Cheltenham 2014. Willie Mullins has hot favourites in four races on Day One, will Irish eyes be smiling again?

Day Two:

The Black Aeroplane: After a runaway success in 2013, Sprinter Sacre missed the defence of his Queen Mother Champion Chase through injury. Can the highest rated jumps horse in training get back on track?

Bumper Success: Irish trained horses have won 17 of the 22 Cheltenham Bumpers – eight of them by Willie Mullins.

Recipe for the RSA: 24 of the last 26 RSA Chase winners had finished first or second last time out.

Day Three:

World Hurdle: It usually pays to look near the top of the market - no horse higher than 8/1 starting price has won the race since 2000.

Frequent Flyers: All bar one of the nine Ryanair Chase winners had previously won a race at Cheltenham.

JLT Clues: The festivals’ newest race, the JLT Novices’ Chase has been won by previous winners of Listed or Graded races over hurdles aged seven or under while three of the four winners have been trained in Ireland. Last year’s Supreme Novices’ winner Vautour head the market.

Day Four:

Beware the Triumph: Only one favourite has won the Triumph Hurdle in the last 19 years - Detroit City for Philip Hobbs in 2006.

Chasing Gold: Just one horse – Kauto Star – has regained the Gold Cup. Nicky Henderson’s Bobs Worth attempts to match him this year.

Albert Bartlett for AP: AP McCoy has won the Albert Bartlett Novices’ Hurdle three times since its inception in 2005 – more than any other jockey.
